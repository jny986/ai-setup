# ISO 24495-1 plain-language reference for agents

Use this reference when planning, writing or reviewing a document against ISO 24495-1:2023, *Plain language — Part 1: Governing principles and guidelines*. It paraphrases the [published preview](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/78907/d194fac21d6a45f38bfcfec9657f7498/ISO-24495-1-2023.pdf). Clause numbers identify the corresponding material in the standard.

**Coverage:** the preview includes the introduction, clauses 1–4, all of 5.1, and 5.2 through the first set of techniques in 5.2.3. It does not include the remaining guideline text or annexes. Consult the complete standard for those details; this reference alone cannot establish conformity.

## Purpose and scope

Plain language succeeds when the intended audience can locate, comprehend and act on the information. Judge the document by the reader's success, rather than treating a readability score as proof of clarity.

The standard concerns printed and digital material whose main form is text. Its audience includes creators of public, technical, legal and controlled-language documents. Its principles apply across written languages; the preview's examples are in English. Separate technical accessibility guidance is still needed for accessible digital documents.

Plain language and easy language serve different needs. Easy language specifically supports people who have difficulties with reading comprehension; an ordinary plain-language review does not establish that those needs are met.

## Readers and evaluation (clause 3)

A reader is any intended user of the document. Include people who scan for a particular answer, hear the text read aloud, or use another way to access it. When audiences have conflicting needs, prioritize the primary audience.

Information design brings together text, typography, visuals and multimedia to support the reader's task. Evaluation examines whether people can locate, comprehend and apply the information.

## Four governing principles (clause 4)

| Principle | Review question |
|---|---|
| Relevant | Does the document supply the information this audience needs for its purpose? |
| Findable | Can the audience locate the needed information easily? |
| Understandable | Can the audience interpret that information easily and accurately? |
| Usable | Can the audience apply the information to accomplish its purpose? |

Apply the principles together. They influence each other throughout development. Relevance, organization and clarity make successful use more likely; ongoing evaluation is needed to establish whether the document actually works for readers.

## Relevance: audience, purpose and content (5.1)

Establish the audience and its needs before drafting. Choose the medium and information to fit the reader's situation.

| Clause | Agent action |
|---|---|
| 5.1.2: Audience | Identify intended readers, their subject knowledge, literacy, language skills and cultural backgrounds. Include accessibility needs and preferences. Use languages the readers understand and account for applicable language requirements. |
| 5.1.3: Purpose | Identify what readers want to accomplish: perform a task, make a decision, understand a subject, recognize an expected action, pursue an interest or gain necessary knowledge. |
| 5.1.4: Context | Consider location, reading technology, available time, attention, interest and emotional state. The document type can affect these conditions. |
| 5.1.5: Document type | Choose a format that suits the audience, purpose and context. Consider another medium when it serves the purpose better. |
| 5.1.6: Content | Answer the readers' questions and include the information needed for their task. Account for the author's legitimate purposes while keeping the audience's needs central. |

For audience evidence, use prior communications, interviews, surveys or relevant research. Distinguish evidence from assumptions when the audience has not been studied.

Select content accurately and ethically. Remove material the audience does not need, while retaining information necessary for an informed understanding or decision. Include required information without misleading readers or concealing facts they need.

## Findability: structure and design (available parts of 5.2)

Readers should be able to recognize the document's subject and decide whether it serves their purpose. Structure and presentation help them locate an answer; headings can help them anticipate subsequent content.

### Organization (5.2.2)

- Keep related information together and order it according to the audience's needs.
- Put the main message where it is easy to locate, often near the start.
- Build unfamiliar information on what readers already know.
- Arrange instructions and processes in execution order.
- Put information needed by most readers before information needed by a smaller group.
- Place relevant warnings before instructions when mistakes could cause harm or damage.

### Visual relationships (5.2.3, available portion)

| Technique | Agent action |
|---|---|
| Prominence | Make important content visually noticeable, for example through size or emphasis. |
| Proximity | Position related elements near one another. |
| Similarity | Give elements with the same function a consistent appearance. |

The preview stops here. These three techniques are the available portion of 5.2.3, rather than its complete guidance.

## Understanding and use: available guidance

Clause 4 establishes understanding and successful use as essential outcomes. The preview's contents page lists later guidelines on words, sentences, paragraphs, visuals, tone, cohesion and evaluation. Their detailed provisions are outside the preview and are not summarized here.

For a review based on the available material, assess whether the intended audience can interpret the information and accomplish its purpose. Treat this as an application of the governing principles; consult the complete standard before attributing a specific technique to clauses 5.3 or 5.4.

## Agent review checklist

This checklist operationalizes the available guidance. Its reporting conventions are workflow choices, not additional ISO requirements.

1. **Establish the reader's task.** Record the primary audience, purpose, context and evidence supporting those choices. Identify assumptions that could affect the result.
2. **Check relevance.** Account for the questions readers need answered, required content and facts needed for informed decisions. Remove unnecessary material without losing essential information.
3. **Check findability.** Review the main message, grouping, sequence and visual relationships. Check that risk information appears before the affected action.
4. **Assess understanding and use.** Evaluate the document throughout development. Where reader evaluation is available, record what it demonstrates about the intended task. Keep editorial judgment distinct from observed reader performance.
5. **Report coverage.** State which material was reviewed, what evidence was available and what remains uncertain. Describe a preview-based review as such; reserve claims about the complete standard for a review against its complete provisions.
