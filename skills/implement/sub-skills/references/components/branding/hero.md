# Hero

## Scope

A hero establishes the page subject, primary value or task, and next action in the first viewport. The component must be shaped by page type: a product homepage, article, pricing page, tool, and authenticated application do not share one hero formula.

## Context Decisions

- Product and landing pages usually need a literal offer, supporting explanation, primary action, and product evidence.
- Tool pages may place the real input or primary interaction in the hero.
- Articles need title, dek, authorship, date, and media appropriate to editorial hierarchy.
- Application screens often need a compact page header instead of a marketing hero.

## Variants

- Product evidence: product UI or real output is the visual anchor.
- Full-bleed media: suitable when place, product, person, or experience can be inspected through strong media.
- Task-first: the usable input or action is the primary experience.
- Editorial: title and publication metadata lead; decoration remains subordinate.

## Constraints

- The headline names the product, page subject, or literal offer; supporting copy carries the detailed value proposition.
- Media must reveal the actual subject rather than act as generic atmosphere.
- Hero height and crop preserve access to the primary action and a clear continuation at supported viewport sizes.
- Calls to action must reflect available behavior and real destinations.
- The hero's containment follows the page hierarchy and established design system.

## Acceptance criteria

The subject and primary action remain clear at mobile and desktop widths. Long copy, media crops, loading, heading hierarchy, and the transition to the next section preserve that hierarchy; the action reaches its promised destination.
